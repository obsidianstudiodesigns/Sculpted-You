Semaglutide, tirzepatide or retatrutide?

They're siblings: all three act on GLP-1, the fullness hormone. Each newer one adds a target. Here's how they differ, and why you might choose one over another.

Which one, and why

Why choose retatrutide instead of tirzepatide?

Retatrutide adds a third target, glucagon, which raises the energy your body burns and helps clear liver fat. In trials it produced the largest average weight loss of the three. It's the newest, though, with the least long-term safety data, and it can raise your heart rate. People usually consider it with a high BMI or after a plateau on tirzepatide.

Why choose semaglutide instead of tirzepatide or retatrutide?

Semaglutide has the longest track record and the most real-world experience. It works on one target, so it's the gentlest place to start and the most affordable. Many people reach their goal on semaglutide alone and never need to step up.

Why choose tirzepatide instead of semaglutide?

Tirzepatide adds GIP to GLP-1. In head-to-head trials it gave more weight loss than semaglutide, and many people find the stomach side effects easier at the same stage. It's a common next step for anyone who stalls on semaglutide or has insulin resistance.

Can I switch between them?

People do move from one to another, usually upward when results slow. A switch should be planned with your doctor, because the starting amount on the new peptide matters.

Side effects all three share

  • Nausea, especially in the first weeks and after dose increases
  • Diarrhoea or constipation
  • Bloating, reflux and burping
  • Fatigue, headaches or dizziness while appetite adjusts
  • Low blood pressure in some people
  • Rare but serious: pancreatitis and gallbladder problems. Severe stomach pain needs a doctor
